About me

I help people get comfortable with technology, then do something meaningful with it.

AA

I've always been most interested in what happens where people and technology meet. How do you make a new tool feel approachable and exciting, while helping people understand its realistic limits? And how do you design an experience that leaves people curious enough to keep exploring on their own?

I'm someone who finds so much joy in watching an idea come to life through technology. That's what first drew me to multimedia and new media, and it's the same spark I feel walking through a thoughtfully designed museum exhibit or theme park, where the technology quietly disappears into the experience. When it's done well, it feels a little like magic. Alongside that, service has been part of my life since I was young, from food banks and holiday toy drives to visiting with nursing home residents. I pursued my M.S.Ed. in Educational Technology to bring those passions together, using design and technology to help people find a positive, confident way into something new.

For more than 15 years, that's shaped my work in technology adoption, learning, and experience design. I've taught computer skills to students with individualized learning needs, built role-based training for enterprise software, and supported instructional technology across school districts. Today, as a Senior Facilitator at LHRIC, I partner with districts on AI adoption, Google Workspace and Microsoft 365, and platform enablement, from one-on-one coaching to sessions with more than 100 participants.

Some of my favorite work happens when emerging technology helps people connect with the world around them and the challenges it faces, like those at the heart of the UN Sustainable Development Goals. Augmented reality can bring a distant place or issue into the room, interactive walls and floors can turn a space into something to explore, and 3D printers and laser cutters let learners design real solutions to problems they care about. That's when technology feels less like a tool and more like a way to make a positive impact.

What I enjoy most is translating between the people who use technology and the teams who build and support it. That's a big part of why I'm excited about where learning experiences are headed. AI, immersive tools, and smarter platforms are changing how and where people learn, and I'd love to keep growing alongside the teams building them, helping the people on the other side of the screen feel capable and curious from the very first click.

How I approach the work

Start small, then scale

I like to pilot first. When I introduced a new LMS, I tried it in my own classroom, reviewed results with leadership, expanded to a few interested teachers, and only then rolled it out to the whole school.

Learn the system, not just the buttons

Whether it's warehouse software customized for each client or a district's Google Workspace environment, I stay close to the people who build and support the technology so training reflects how things actually work.

Build for independence

Good support means people need me less over time. I coach department leaders to edit their own web pages, and years ago I trained a school director to update the website I rebuilt.

What I'm excited about next

Emerging technology is opening up new ways to learn, and I think the most interesting work is happening where great products and great learning experiences meet.

AI as a thinking partner

I've watched AI go from a curiosity to part of everyday workflows. What excites me is designing experiences that help people use it thoughtfully and with confidence, not just quickly.

Experiences that feel immersive

The most memorable experiences are the ones people step into rather than just watch. From augmented reality to VR, I'd love to help design learning that invites people in and makes new ideas feel real.

Learning built into the product

Some of the best learning happens inside the tool itself. I'm curious about in-product guidance and just-in-time support that meets people right when they need it.
